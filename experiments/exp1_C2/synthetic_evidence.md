## 2. Occlusion recovery (fragmentation / ID switches)

| scenario | baseline (linear) | CTRV |
|---|---|---|
| constant turn, 12-frame gap | frags=1, **idsw=1** | frags=1, **idsw=0** |
| weaving, 2×8-frame gaps | frags=2, idsw=0 | frags=2, idsw=1 |

**Reading:** on a sustained turn the linear tangent extrapolation drifts off
the arc during the gap and the track re-identifies (IDSW); CTRV coasts along
the arc and keeps the identity. This is the C2 claim.

**Documented boundary (for the paper's analysis section):** the weaving gap
was deliberately placed right after a turn-rate **sign flip** — the filter's
ω estimate lags (~10 frames) and points the wrong way at gap start, so any
curvilinear extrapolation loses to a lucky straight coast there by one IDSW.
Verified robust across Q_theta ∈ [1e-3, 5e-2], Q_omega ∈ [1e-4, 5e-3],
R ∈ [2, 10], and ω-decay λ ∈ {1.0, 0.9, 0.8, 0.6, 0.0} during lost coasting —
no setting rescues that gap without hurting the RMSE table. Chosen operating
point: Q = diag(0.5, 0.5, 0.1, 1e-3, 1e-4), R = diag(10, 10), full CTRV
dynamics while lost (ω retained).

## Implementation notes worth keeping

- Heading bootstrap (θ, v set directly from the first displacement) must
  also clear the corresponding rows/columns of P — resetting only the
  diagonal leaves stale cross-covariances (|corr| > 1) and P loses positive
  definiteness on the next update (found by `test_P_stays_symmetric_...`).
- The scaffold's placeholder Q (θ-noise 0.1 rad² ≈ 18°/frame) drowned the
  heading estimate in process noise; CTRV was *worse* than linear until Q
  was retuned.
- ByteTrack's lost-track analog: the linear KF keeps its positional
  velocities when lost (only zeroes the aspect velocity mean[7]); CTRV
  correspondingly keeps v, θ, ω and coasts along the arc.
